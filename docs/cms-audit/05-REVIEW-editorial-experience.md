# CleanStart CMS Review — Editorial & Authoring Experience

How it actually feels to create and publish content: articles, events/resources/jobs, the page builder, and taxonomy/people/media management.

This review judges the CMS from the editor's chair. The engineering underneath is consistently strong — conditional fields fire correctly, validation lands at the right boundaries, JSON-LD builders are thorough, and several components (the SEO sidebar, the media pipeline, the FAQ bulk-paste) are genuinely better than anything stock Payload ships. The recurring weakness is the *editorial* layer on top of that engineering: sidebars that scroll forever before the Publish button, forms overloaded with arrays a working writer will never fill, microcopy that leaks developer notes, and a handful of fields wired to sources that don't exist. None of it is broken in a way that blocks shipping content; all of it is the kind of friction that compounds across hundreds of publishes.

## Scorecard

| Area | Score | Takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| Article-type content (Blogs, News, Guides, KnowledgeBase) | 7/10 | Best-in-class SEO and publish tooling, dragged down by a ~20-item sidebar and Guide-form overload. |
| Events, Webinars, Podcasts, Resources, Jobs, JobLocations | 6/10 | Solid technical skeleton with real gaps: dead fields, broken SEO sources, underspecified podcast workflow. |
| Pages block builder + block library | 7/10 | Excellent per-block field design undermined by a spartan, preview-less, collapse-less builder shell. |
| Taxonomy, People, Galleries & Media | 6.5/10 | A standout media pipeline alongside three duplicate taxonomies, an over-modelled author CV, and dead picker code. |

**Average score: 6.6/10.**

---

## Article-type content (Blogs, News, Guides, KnowledgeBase)

**Score: 7/10**

The CMS delivers a genuinely sophisticated content-authoring environment. The SEO sidebar is best-in-class for a custom Payload build: auto-synced meta title/description, pixel-accurate SERP preview, live JSON-LD validation, a publish health score, and a carefully tiered progressive-disclosure structure that keeps expert knobs hidden by default. The publish workflow — split-button, schedule dialog with timezone support, DisplayPublishedAt with contextual backdate warnings — is well above the Payload default.

However, the overall sidebar length for a Blog article is overloaded: roughly 20 discrete sidebar items scroll the editor past a dozen collapsed cards before reaching the Publish button. The Guides collection adds a compounding burden of articleSections array, HowTo schema group, citations array, and keywords array in the main body — creating a form that a working writer would find overwhelming for a typical 1,200-word post. The News collection lacks a top-level abstract field despite the SEO description auto-syncing from it (inconsistent modeling). The FaqBulkPaste feature is elegant but uses fragile DOM-traversal with staggered setTimeout arrays to fight Payload's focus management — a sign of implementation pressure that will break on a Payload upgrade. The previousPost/nextPost pagination model puts symmetric-link maintenance burden on the editor and the JourneyMirrorWarning only diagnoses, never fixes.

**Strengths**

- Auto-synced SEO title and description fields (Yoast-style manual-mode toggle) eliminate a common data-entry chore without hiding the override affordance.
- Pixel-budget SERP preview using canvas `measureText` genuinely mirrors Google's truncation rather than guessing at character counts.
- Live JSON-LD schema health badge with per-check expand, Rich Results link, and admin-only override ingest is more capable than most commercial CMSes.
- Schedule publish dialog with type/time/timezone selection and an upcoming-events table with delete is complete and well-designed.
- FaqBulkPaste bulk-split on paste with auto-focus is a high-value ergonomic shortcut for editors importing Q&A content.
- SEO Advanced panel correctly defers robots directives and speakable selectors behind double progressive disclosure — most editors never need to touch it.
- BodyAuditField giving internal/external link counts, alt-coverage, readability band, and featured-snippet candidates in the sidebar is a strong at-a-glance signal.
- PublishChecklistBanner provides actionable pre-publish feedback with severity-tiered blockers vs. warnings and updates on save.
- DisplayPublishedAtField contextual messaging (near-now / backdate soft+hard / future-draft) with an inline schedule-from-here button is thoughtful and prevents SEO-harmful date manipulation.

**Findings**

### [MAJOR] Blog sidebar has ~20 discrete items — severe scroll depth before Publish  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/fields/seo.ts, apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Blogs.ts` — **What:** `seoSidebarFields()` contributes 11 sidebar items (SEO Health Score, Meta Title, Meta Description, Indexable, SERP Preview, Schema JSON-LD, Canonical URL, Social Card, Redirect, Hreflang, URL Change History), `seoFieldsForSidebar()` adds the hidden SEO group + SEO Advanced panel, and Blogs adds slug, permalink, displayPublishedAt, journeyMirrorWarning, tocDepth, featured, pinned — totaling roughly 20 sidebar cards. Even with most collapsed by default, the sidebar DOM is enormous and the Publish control is buried near the bottom. A writer publishing their fourth post in a day will scroll past 15 collapsed cards on every save. — **Recommendation:** Move URL Change History, Hreflang, and Redirect out of the article sidebar and into a dedicated per-document 'URL & Redirects' tab or a separate view. These are touched < 5% of publishes. Consider grouping featured/pinned/tocDepth into a single 'Display options' collapsible to reduce card count by 2. Target a maximum of 12 expanded+collapsed items in the article sidebar.

### [MAJOR] Guides collection has 6 main-body arrays/groups beyond the body field — writer overwhelm  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Guides.ts` — **What:** After the rich-text body, a Guide editor is presented with: FaqBulkPaste + FAQs array, articleSections array (each with its own richText field), howTo group (with 4 conditional duration fields), citations array (label + source + URL), and keywords array. That is five distinct post-body sections requiring active management. The articleSections array duplicates the function of headings in the body richText — an editor writing a procedural guide must decide whether to write their how-to steps as body H2s or as separate articleSection rows, and there is no guidance to resolve this tension. — **Recommendation:** Collapse articleSections into a sidebar-only field used exclusively for HowTo schema step labels, with a tooltip explaining the distinction from body content. Alternatively, auto-derive HowTo steps from H2 headings in the body and eliminate the articleSections array entirely. Move keywords array behind a 'Schema extras' collapsible with `initCollapsed: true`. Citations are already collapsed; keep them but add a character hint on the label field.

### [MODERATE] News collection has no 'abstract' label or help text, yet SEO description syncs from it  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/News.ts` — **What:** News.ts defines `{ name: 'abstract', type: 'textarea' }` at line 44 with no label, no description, and no char hint, despite the field being the primary SEO description source (`seoSidebarFields descriptionSource: 'abstract'`). Blog and KnowledgeBase both provide a description hint ('Drives the SEO description fallback. Aim for ≤ 160 characters.') and the ABSTRACT_CHAR_HINT constant. A News editor has no indication that the abstract feeds the meta description or why it matters. — **Recommendation:** Add the same description hint to News.abstract: 'Drives the SEO description fallback. Aim for ≤ 160 characters.' and add `ABSTRACT_CHAR_HINT = 160` to News.ts for consistency. Three lines of change.

### [MODERATE] FaqBulkPaste uses fragile DOM-walk and staggered setTimeout to fight Payload focus management  (value-vs-complexity · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/FaqBulkPaste.tsx` — **What:** The auto-focus-on-add-row logic uses a MutationObserver on `document.body` plus a staggered retry sequence at `[120, 260, 450, 700, 1000, 1400]`ms to win a race against Payload's own focus management. The Portal injection into Payload's array-field header UL uses three staggered setTimeout calls (100, 500, 1500ms) plus another MutationObserver. This is five async timers and two document-level observers active simultaneously whenever a FAQ array is visible. Any Payload upgrade that changes the array-field DOM structure (class names, UL presence, row selectors) will silently break auto-focus and the Clear-all portal injection. — **Recommendation:** Use a stable CSS data attribute (e.g. `data-field-name='faqs'`) on the array root instead of brittle class selectors. Replace the staggered setTimeout focus loop with a single ResizeObserver or `queueMicrotask`+`requestAnimationFrame` combination — or simply accept that the new-row focus is a polish feature and remove the retry loop, keeping only the 120ms initial attempt. The portal injection for Clear-all is the higher-value feature; verify it against Payload's array-field snapshot tests after each upgrade.

### [MODERATE] previousPost/nextPost on Blogs puts symmetric-link maintenance on the editor with no fix affordance  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Blogs.ts, apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/JourneyMirrorWarning.tsx` — **What:** An editor who sets Post A's nextPost to Post B must also open Post B and set its previousPost back to Post A. JourneyMirrorWarning correctly detects the asymmetry and surfaces a yellow callout — but the callout is read-only. The editor must click the linked post title (opens a new tab), navigate to the other post, find the field, and fix it manually. For a content team publishing 3–5 posts per week, this creates a non-obvious two-document edit ritual for every new 'journey' pair. — **Recommendation:** Add a one-click 'Fix mirror' button to JourneyMirrorWarning that calls `PATCH /api/blogs/:targetId` with the correct mirror field set. The component already has all the data needed: currentId, targetId, and direction. Alternatively, consider a server hook on Blogs afterChange that automatically writes the mirror back — this eliminates the editor burden entirely and the warning becomes a consistency check rather than an action prompt.

### [MODERATE] Schema (JSON-LD) preview card shows 'Draft' state and no preview for unpublished docs — creates publish-then-check loop  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/SchemaPreviewField.tsx` — **What:** SchemaPreviewField lines 184–191 show 'Schema is generated only for published documents. Save and publish to see the JSON-LD that will be emitted.' This means a new-article author cannot verify their JSON-LD is correct before hitting Publish. The admin-only preview path (lines 182–195) only bypasses this when the user has an admin role AND has set an additionalSchema override — it does not preview the auto-emitted schema for a draft. An editor who sets up a guide's HowTo group, citations, and keywords has no way to confirm the schema emitted is correct until they publish or unpublish-to-correct. — **Recommendation:** For admin users specifically, call the preview endpoint with the current form state regardless of publish status. The endpoint already exists (`/api/jsonld/:collection/:id`, POST with additionalSchema). Extend it to accept a full draft-preview payload, or add a separate `/api/jsonld/:collection/:id/preview` endpoint that accepts the unsaved form fields (similar to how Payload's draft preview works). Non-admin editors can continue to see the 'Draft — publish to preview' message.

### [MINOR] SchedulePublishDialog re-creates a duplicate entry point next to CmsPublishButton — two schedule affordances visible simultaneously  (consistency-states · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/CmsPublishButton.tsx, apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/views/edit/PublishMenu.tsx` — **What:** CmsPublishButton (registered globally) provides a Publish split-button with a 'Schedule publish...' chevron. PublishMenu (mounted per-collection via docStatusBarEditConfig) is a separate split-button that also exposes 'Schedule publish...' plus Save Draft and Unpublish. Both are mounted on content collections simultaneously. An editor sees two chevron-menus near the top of the edit view with overlapping scheduling affordances. The interaction surface for 'how do I schedule this post?' is split across two components. — **Recommendation:** Determine whether CmsPublishButton or PublishMenu is the canonical publish surface for content collections and remove the other. Given PublishMenu handles Save Draft + Unpublish which CmsPublishButton does not, PublishMenu is the richer component. CmsPublishButton was likely an earlier iteration that should be retired for content collections (keeping it only for collections without versions).

### [MINOR] KeywordTargetField placement is ambiguous — it reads seo.keywordTarget but renders in the SEO band with no visible grouping boundary  (ia-navigation · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/KeywordTargetField.tsx, apps/cms/src/payload/fields/seo.ts` — **What:** The KeywordTargetField is mounted via seoSidebarFields which places it above SEO Health Score in the render order (seoSidebarFields prepends seoHealthScore first, then seoTitle, seoDescription, seoIndexable, serpPreview, schemaPreview, canonicalUrl, socialCard, redirect, headTags, urlChangeHistory). However KeywordTargetField is not listed in seoSidebarFields — it is referenced from the `fields/seo.ts` hidden keywordTargetField definition but there is no explicit KeywordTargetField UI field in seoSidebarFields. The target keyword input therefore appears to be absent from the sidebar despite the underlying data field existing. An editor has no direct editing surface for `seo.keywordTarget` unless another mount point exists. — **Recommendation:** Audit whether KeywordTargetField is actually mounted anywhere on content collections. If it is absent, add it explicitly to seoSidebarFields between BodyAuditField and SeoHealthScoreField — this is the natural position (write → audit body → check keyword density → check overall SEO health). If it is mounted elsewhere, document the mount point in the field definition.

### [MINOR] tocDepth control misleads: 'Re-save to apply' instruction is confusing in an autosave environment  (microcopy · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Blogs.ts (line 227), apps/cms/src/payload/collections/KnowledgeBase.ts` — **What:** The tocDepth field description says 'Heading levels that appear in the Table of Contents. Re-save to apply.' On collections with autosave enabled (versions.drafts with schedulePublish), 're-save' is ambiguous — does autosave trigger the bodyStatsHook? Does the editor need to manually save draft? The instruction will cause editors to click 'Save Draft' unnecessarily if autosave already fires the hook, or miss that they need to do so if it does not. — **Recommendation:** Replace 'Re-save to apply' with 'Takes effect on the next save. The TOC below updates automatically.' If autosave does trigger bodyStatsHook, make this explicit. If it does not (autosave might debounce differently), consider triggering the TOC rebuild client-side on tocDepth change so the collapsible TOC preview updates immediately without a round-trip.

### [MINOR] HowTo schema group uses free-text ISO 8601 duration fields with no format validation or helper  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Guides.ts (lines 147-186)` — **What:** The howTo group exposes totalTime, prepTime, performTime as plain text fields with a description example ('e.g. PT30M = 30 minutes, PT1H30M = 1 hour 30 min'). There is no client-side validation of the ISO 8601 duration format, no picker component, and no feedback if an editor types '30 minutes' (invalid) instead of 'PT30M'. Invalid durations will silently produce malformed HowTo schema that Google's Rich Results tester will reject. — **Recommendation:** Add a Zod `validate()` callback on each duration field using a simple ISO 8601 duration regex (`/^P(?:\d+Y)?(?:\d+M)?(?:\d+W)?(?:\d+D)?(?:T(?:\d+H)?(?:\d+M)?(?:\d+S)?)?$/`) with a helpful error message that includes the correct format. Alternatively, replace the three text fields with a numeric duration picker (hours + minutes selects) that serializes to ISO 8601 on save.

### [MINOR] relatedPosts on Blogs allows pinning up to 3 posts but no explicit max validation enforces this  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Blogs.ts (lines 145-153)` — **What:** The relatedPosts description says 'Pin up to 3 posts the reader should see next' but `hasMany: true` with no maxRows constraint. An editor can add 10 related posts and the description's '3' guidance is purely advisory. The rendering layer presumably truncates to 3, but the admin gives no feedback that posts beyond position 3 will be ignored. — **Recommendation:** Add `maxRows: 3` to the relatedPosts field admin config. This surfaces a built-in Payload constraint that reflects the business rule and prevents editor confusion when their carefully-chosen post #4 silently disappears on the front end.

### [MINOR] SchemaOverrideModal in SchemaPreviewField is a raw portal with inline styles — inconsistent with the @cleanstart/ui Dialog used everywhere else  (visual-design · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/SchemaPreviewField.tsx (lines 750-973)` — **What:** The SchemaOverrideModal renders as a custom portal with 250+ lines of inline style objects and a raw `<dialog>` element, bypassing the Dialog/DialogHeader/DialogBody/DialogFooter primitives from @cleanstart/ui that every other modal in the admin uses (SchedulePublishDialog, ConfirmDialog, etc.). The visual result is inconsistent — different border-radius, button styling, backdrop opacity, and no focus-trap from the shared Dialog primitive. The `aria-modal='true'` is correct but the modal lacks an explicit onKeyDown Escape only at the dialog level; backdrop clicks have no close handler. — **Recommendation:** Refactor SchemaOverrideModal to use the Dialog/DialogHeader/DialogBody/DialogFooter primitives from @cleanstart/ui. The raw file upload and textarea content stays; only the chrome wrapping changes. This removes ~60 lines of inline style and gets focus-trap and backdrop-dismiss for free.

## Events, Webinars, Podcasts, Resources, Jobs, JobLocations

**Score: 6/10**

These six collections show careful engineering fundamentals — conditional-field patterns are technically correct, validation fires at the right boundaries, JSON-LD builders are thorough, and the event lifecycle automation (cancelledAt, previousStartDate) is genuinely useful. However, the surface contains several meaningful authoring gaps that would frustrate a real content team: Webinars and Events diverge in ways that will confuse editors who manage both; on-demand webinars inherit live-event UI that doesn't apply; Jobs carries a dead readOnly field (closedAt) that will never be populated because its stamp hook was never written; and PodcastEpisodes is underspecified for a real show workflow. The conditional-required workaround in Resources (GateFormLabel) is clever but highlights a Payload limitation that may surprise new editors. Overall: solid technical skeleton, incomplete editorial polish.

**Strengths**

- The registrationMode discriminator (internal/external) with per-branch conditional validation is well-executed — both Events and Webinars show/hide the right fields and block save with specific error messages when the paired field is absent.
- The eventStatusTimestampsHook for Events and Webinars (auto-stamping cancelledAt, capturing previousStartDate on postpone) is a thoughtful automation that prevents editors from forgetting SEO-critical Schema.org signals; the hook is also unit-tested with clear edge-case coverage.
- Resource gating is modeled simply and correctly: a single checkbox gates the download, a relationship picks the form, and a conditional required-asterisk (GateFormLabel) gives a clear visual cue despite Payload's required-validation limitation.
- The postEventCta group on Events (enabled checkbox + label + URL with interdependent validation) is a clean, low-friction pattern for the very common 'event is over, now show a recording link' use case.
- PodcastEpisodes' YouTube URL normalization is a well-scoped quality-of-life feature — accepting any of five URL shapes and stamping a stable 11-char ID means the front end never has to re-parse, and the validation error message is specific enough to guide editors.

**Findings**

### [MAJOR] Webinars missing postEventCta equivalent; on-demand type still shows live-event fields  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Webinars.ts` — **What:** Events has a postEventCta group (enabled + label + URL) and a ctaLabel override for the registration button. Webinars has neither, despite an identical need: after a live webinar ends, editors want to surface a recording link, and they may also want a custom register CTA. Additionally, the registrationMode select (required, defaultValue 'external') is shown unconditionally regardless of webinarType — an on-demand replay has no registration concept, yet the editor must still pick a mode and fill a URL or face a validation error. The eventStatus (Scheduled/Postponed/Cancelled) also renders for on-demand webinars, which is logically meaningless for content that has no date. — **Recommendation:** Add a postEventCta group to Webinars mirroring Events (the pattern is already proven). Add a ctaLabel field. Suppress registrationMode, registrationUrl, registrationForm, attendeesCap, and eventStatus when `webinarType === 'on-demand'` using `admin.condition` — on-demand entries need only recordingUrl and slidesUrl.

### [MAJOR] Jobs closedAt field is permanently null — stamp hook was never written  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Jobs.ts` — **What:** closedAt is declared readOnly with `access: { update: () => false }` and is shown in the sidebar only when `hiringStatus === 'closed'`. However, no beforeChange hook stamps it, and no cron job writes to it. The analogous cancelledAt on Events is correctly auto-stamped by eventStatusTimestampsHook. closedAt will always display as empty, making it a misleading audit trail field. The expiresAt field description also references 'the auto-close cron (Phase G)' but that cron file does not exist in `apps/cms/src/payload/jobs/`. — **Recommendation:** Write a jobStatusTimestampsHook mirroring eventStatusTimestampsHook: stamp `closedAt = now` on the hiringStatus 'open'→'closed' or 'paused'→'closed' transition, clear it if status moves back to 'open'. Register it in the Jobs beforeChange array. Either deliver the auto-close cron or update the expiresAt description to say the feature is planned.

### [MAJOR] Jobs SEO descriptionSource references a field ('abstract') that does not exist  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Jobs.ts` — **What:** The seoSidebarFields call passes `descriptionSource: 'abstract'`, but the Jobs collection has no field named 'abstract' — only a body richText and no textarea short summary. The SeoDescriptionField and SerpPreview sidebar components will find nothing to mirror, meaning every job's SEO description field will always auto-fill as empty and the SERP preview will show a blank snippet. The editor has no visible prompt to write a description at all. — **Recommendation:** Either add a required 'summary' textarea field to Jobs (paralleling Resources' 'summary') and change descriptionSource to 'summary', or rename the reference to 'body' if the richText extraction utility supports that. A short-summary textarea is the better option for jobs because richText body content is too long for a 160-char meta description.

### [MODERATE] Webinars registrationMode field has no description; Events' description leaks implementation language  (microcopy · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Webinars.ts` — **What:** The Webinars registrationMode field has no `admin.description` at all. Events' description reads 'Per-record switchable per locked schema decision.' — this is a developer note, not editor guidance. It tells the editor nothing about what the choice means or when to use each option. First-time editors will not understand the difference between 'In-house form' and 'External URL' without external documentation. — **Recommendation:** Replace with editor-facing copy on both: 'In-house form: visitors register through a Payload-managed form (leads are captured in CMS). External URL: registration is handled by an external service like Zoom, Eventbrite, or LinkedIn (no leads captured in CMS).'

### [MODERATE] Timezone field is a free-text box with no validation or picker — silent fallback on bad input  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Events.ts` — **What:** Both Events and Webinars accept timezone as a free-text field with only a description hint: 'IANA timezone string (e.g. Asia/Kolkata)'. There is no validation against the IANA timezone database. An editor who types 'IST', 'EST', 'UTC+5:30', or 'India' will silently get the siteSettings.organizationTimezone fallback — and the rendered event time will be wrong, potentially with no visible error. The same issue exists on Webinars (shorter description, no example). — **Recommendation:** Add a validate function that checks the value against `Intl.supportedValuesOf('timeZone')` (available in Node 22+) if non-empty, returning a specific error like 'Not a recognised IANA timezone. Try Asia/Kolkata, America/New_York, Europe/London.' As a longer-term improvement, replace the text field with a searchable select using the Intl API to enumerate valid values.

### [MODERATE] Resources accessLevel defaultValue 'public' is stale when gated is toggled on then off  (consistency-states · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Resources.ts` — **What:** accessLevel has `defaultValue: 'public'` but is only visible when `gated === true`. When an editor checks gated, the field appears — but if Payload had already stored 'lead-gated' from a previous save, unchecking gated hides the field but leaves 'lead-gated' in the database invisibly. The front-end renderer must not rely on accessLevel alone to gate downloads, but the data inconsistency can cause confusion in analytics and reporting. Also: gated is `required=false` with `defaultValue: false`, but accessLevel is not reset when gated is unchecked. — **Recommendation:** Add a beforeChange hook on Resources that sets `accessLevel = 'public'` whenever gated is false. Alternatively, derive accessLevel entirely from gated + gateForm presence on read and remove the explicit field, since the 'customer-only' option has no enforcement mechanism and appears aspirational.

### [MODERATE] PodcastEpisodes missing key editorial fields for a real show workflow  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/PodcastEpisodes.ts` — **What:** The collection models a YouTube-hosted podcast episode but omits several fields a real show workflow needs: (1) no guests/speakers relationship to Authors — only an episodeNumber and abstract; (2) no showNotes richText field for the full episode description rendered on the detail page; (3) no host field — the podcast host is not attributable per episode; (4) no transcript relationship or text field; (5) no season field — the collection has no concept of multi-season structure, relying purely on episodeNumber for order. The abstract is capped at ~240 characters per the description, which is a card-level blurb but cannot serve as the full detail-page body. — **Recommendation:** Add a speakers relationship to Authors (hasMany), a showNotes richText field for the episode detail page body, and an optional season number. A transcript field (textarea or richText) is lower priority but improves accessibility. The showNotes field is the most urgent — without it there is no body content for the detail page.

### [MINOR] durationSeconds on PodcastEpisodes requires editors to do mental math  (authoring-ux · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/PodcastEpisodes.ts` — **What:** The field accepts raw seconds (e.g. 3840) with no conversion aid. The description says 'Total runtime in seconds' but gives no example. An editor timing a 64-minute episode must calculate 3840 manually. There is also no read-only display showing the human-readable equivalent alongside the input. — **Recommendation:** Either change the field to accept HH:MM:SS or H:MM format (text field with validation converting to seconds on save), or add a custom field description that includes a converter hint such as 'e.g. 3840 = 1h 4m 0s. Multiply hours × 3600, minutes × 60, add seconds.' A custom UI field showing the formatted duration next to the input would be the best editorial experience.

### [MINOR] Resources type select is optional with no default — resources routinely publish without a type  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Resources.ts` — **What:** The type field (whitepaper, ebook, datasheet, architecture-insights, report) is not required and has no defaultValue. The ctaButtonText description explicitly references type-based CTA copy fallback ('Whitepapers → Download whitepaper, Reports → Read the report') — but if type is absent the CTA will fall back to a generic string. The listing view and mega-menu filtering will also show untyped resources, degrading UX for site visitors. — **Recommendation:** Make type required (no defaultValue, forcing an explicit choice) or add `defaultValue: 'whitepaper'` with a note in the description. Required is cleaner — every resource belongs to a type.

### [MINOR] Jobs hiringStatus 'paused' has no editorial guidance and applicationDeadline/expiresAt are hidden  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Jobs.ts` — **What:** When hiringStatus is 'paused', both applicationDeadline and expiresAt are hidden (their condition is `hiringStatus === 'open'`), and closedAt is also hidden (condition: `hiringStatus === 'closed'`). An editor switching a listing to Paused loses visibility of when the listing was due to expire and cannot see when it was originally deadline-gated. There is no description on hiringStatus explaining what Paused means operationally ('Applications are temporarily frozen; listing remains visible but CTA is suppressed'). — **Recommendation:** Add a description to hiringStatus explaining each value's front-end effect. Show applicationDeadline as readOnly when status is 'paused' (not hidden) so editors retain the context of when the original deadline was. Consider adding a pausedAt auto-stamped field analogous to closedAt.

### [MINOR] GateFormLabel required asterisk is aria-hidden — screen readers get no cue that the field became required  (accessibility · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/GateFormLabel.tsx` — **What:** When gated is checked, the asterisk appended to the label is `aria-hidden='true'` with only a title attribute. A screen reader user will hear 'Gate Form' with no indication that a selection is now required. The title attribute is not reliably announced on focus by screen readers. The validate error will eventually fire on save, but there is no proactive live announcement. — **Recommendation:** Remove aria-hidden and instead use visually-hidden text that is readable by screen readers: `<span className="sr-only"> (required)</span>` alongside the visible asterisk. Alternatively, combine `aria-required='true'` on the label's associated input by wiring the field's required attribute dynamically via useFormFields if Payload's field API allows it.

### [QUESTION] Events and Webinars have no tags/categories relationship for content discovery  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Events.ts` — **What:** Blogs and Guides use a categories relationship for filtering and mega-menu discovery. Events and Webinars have no equivalent — only webinarType and region on Webinars. A content team running many events will want to filter by topic (e.g. 'Security', 'SBOM', 'Compliance') both in the CMS list view and on the public listing page. Without tags, the only editorial signals are webinarType, region, and eventStatus. — **Recommendation:** Decide whether the existing Categories collection (used by Blogs) is reusable for events, or whether a dedicated EventTags taxonomy is more appropriate. If a single webinar can cover multiple topics, a hasMany relationship to Categories is a one-line addition. If it's a yes, add it; if Events and Webinars will remain ungrouped by topic, document the decision.

### [QUESTION] PodcastEpisodes has no SEO fields — no seoSidebarFields or schemaAddonsField  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/PodcastEpisodes.ts` — **What:** Unlike every other content collection (Events, Webinars, Resources, Jobs, Blogs, Guides), PodcastEpisodes does not include seoSidebarFields or schemaAddonsField. There is no SEO title override, no meta description, no OG image, no SERP preview, and no Schema.org markup (the collection does not call buildEventBlob or any equivalent). For a podcast this also means no AudioObject or PodcastEpisode Schema.org type. If podcast detail pages are eventually indexed, the lack of SEO fields will matter. — **Recommendation:** Add seoSidebarFields and seoFieldsForSidebar to PodcastEpisodes, wiring pathPrefix to the podcast route and descriptionSource to 'abstract'. Add schemaAddonsField. Evaluate whether a Schema.org PodcastEpisode/AudioObject blob builder is needed for the JSON-LD layer.

## Pages block builder + block library

**Score: 7/10**

The CleanStart page builder is a well-structured, coherent system for a B2B SaaS marketing site. The 19-block roster covers the essential patterns (hero, feature grid, pricing, FAQ, testimonials, logo cloud, forms, code, embed, table, jobs) and the shared field primitives (linkField, ctaButtonFields, mediaUploadField) are DRY and type-safe. The Section layout primitive is a pragmatic choice that avoids block proliferation for the most common column pattern.

However, several real authoring pain points exist: the add-block picker is a flat alphabetical list with no descriptions or previews, making block choice opaque for anyone who hasn't memorized the library; the Section block only supports two-column layout with no three-column or asymmetric-column variants; the block chrome has no collapse-by-default behaviour on long pages so editors scroll through wall-of-fields; and the absence of any block-level preview means an editor building a pricing page cannot see what they're constructing until they visit the live-preview URL. The block collection also has two near-duplicate social-proof blocks (Stats vs MetricsBar) whose distinction is not obvious from their labels alone, and the Table block's row-of-cells-of-cells nested array editing is going to be genuinely painful for editors managing a 6-column comparison table.

The overall score is dragged up by the quality of the individual block field designs (good descriptions, sensible defaults, conditional visibility, validate callbacks) and down by the pagebuilder UX layer itself being minimal-to-the-point-of-spartan.

**Strengths**

- Shared primitives (linkField, ctaButtonFields, mediaUploadField) are used consistently across every block — zero field-level duplication, and the typed-link picker (doc/media/url discriminator with per-kind validation) is meaningfully better than a bare URL text field.
- Cycle-detection on the parent relationship in Pages is a rare, production-quality detail that most Payload sites omit and later regret when a CMS crash from circular references hits production.
- The Section block's 'no Section-inside-Section' constraint is explicitly enforced by excluding it from nestableBlocks — prevents the deeply-nested trees that make page-builder data unrenderable and editors confused.
- FAQ block has genuine authoring-level value-adds: FaqRowLabel surfaces the question text on the collapsed row so editors can scan a 15-item FAQ without opening every accordion; the FaqBulkPaste component intercepts a paste event and auto-splits a multi-Q&A block into rows — a real time-saver for teams importing from a Google Doc.
- Pricing block is thorough: billing toggle, currency per tier, feature rows with tooltip and included/excluded state, conditional highlight label — covers the real marketing copy editing patterns without a separate Pricing collection.
- The CodeBlock admin textarea uses monospace styling, pre-wrap, and 14 rows — the admin widget itself is legible for pasting code snippets, unlike the default Payload textarea.
- Embed block validates the URL against a per-provider pattern at field level, so an editor who pastes a Vimeo URL into a YouTube embed slot gets an inline error rather than a broken iframe on the live page.
- The custom BlocksField preserves stable row IDs (`key={row.id ?? block-${i}}`), uses ConfirmDialog for remove (instead of immediate delete), and correctly dispatches Payload form actions so validation, dirty state, and autosave all stay in sync.

**Findings**

### [MAJOR] Block picker is a bare unsorted label-and-slug list with no descriptions or visual cues  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/fields/BlocksField.tsx` — **What:** The 'Add block' dialog renders a flat `<ul>` where each item shows only the singular label and the slug (e.g. 'Feature grid / featureGrid'). There are no descriptions, no category groupings, no icons, and no previews. With 19 blocks available, a new editor has no way to know whether 'Stats' or 'Metrics bar' is the right choice for a given layout need, or how 'Section' differs from adding a block directly. The picker also always appends at the bottom — there is no ability to insert a block at a specific position in the page. — **Recommendation:** Add a short one-sentence description string to each block definition (not just the array `admin.description`) and surface it in the picker under the label. Group blocks into at least three categories: Layout (Section), Content (RichText, CodeBlock, Table, Embed), Marketing (Hero, CTA, FeatureGrid, Pricing, Testimonial, Stats, MetricsBar, FAQ, LogoCloud, IntegrationLogos, Gallery). Add insertion-at-index support to `insertBlock()` so editors can insert between existing blocks rather than always appending.

### [MODERATE] Stats and MetricsBar are near-duplicate social-proof blocks with no clear differentiation  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Stats.ts` — **What:** Stats (heading + array of value/label/sublabel) and MetricsBar (array of label/value + background colour) are structurally almost identical. Stats has a sublabel per metric and an optional heading; MetricsBar has a background variant and no sublabel. Neither has a description explaining when to use which. In practice these will be used interchangeably and inconsistently across pages, producing two slightly different visual treatments for the same semantic content with no editorial guidance to distinguish them. — **Recommendation:** Either merge them into a single Stats/Metrics block with a 'display style' select (bar vs grid), or add a clear `admin.description` to each block explaining the visual difference: 'MetricsBar: a dark/surface horizontal strip for 3–5 KPIs, used between sections. Stats: a grid of large numbers used as a section hero.' This is a 10-minute addition but prevents years of editorial confusion.

### [MODERATE] Section block only supports two-column layout; no three-column or asymmetric split  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Section.ts` — **What:** The Section block's variant select offers only 'Stack (single column)' and 'Two column'. A real marketing site frequently needs three-column grids (e.g. a row of three use-case cards), a 1:2 asymmetric split (sidebar + main content), or a 2:1 split (copy + narrow form). Currently all of these require either the FeatureGrid block (which is purpose-built for grid cards, not arbitrary content) or a custom one-off page. The Section block is supposed to be the general-purpose layout primitive — its current 2-variant range limits it to the simplest case. — **Recommendation:** Add 'Three column', '1/3 + 2/3 (narrow left)', and '2/3 + 1/3 (narrow right)' variants. These map to CSS grid column templates and require no new rendering logic. Also consider adding a 'padding' select (none/sm/md/lg) mirroring the Section component in apps/web so block-rendered sections can control their own spacing independently.

### [MODERATE] No block-level preview: editors are blind to page composition during authoring  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Pages.ts` — **What:** Pages has a permalink sidebar field and presumably a live-preview URL (via the docStatusBarEditConfig), but block rows themselves show only their raw form fields — there is no inline thumbnail, visual hint, or block-type illustration. Composing a 12-block product page requires either memorizing what each block looks like or repeatedly opening the preview in a browser tab. The block picker suffers the same blindness: 'Testimonial' and 'CTA' are just text labels with no visual differentiation. — **Recommendation:** For the picker, a small (120×80) representative screenshot or hand-drawn SVG per block type is worth the investment and is the standard in Sanity, Contentful, and Builder.io. For block rows, even a coloured left-border accent per block category (layout vs content vs marketing) and a subtle preview headline extracted from the block data (e.g. the Hero headline value, the FAQ count, the Testimonial person name) in the `cs-blocks__row-type` span would give editors meaningful visual landmarks without requiring a full preview iframe.

### [MODERATE] Table block requires editing a deeply nested row-of-cells-of-cells structure, making real comparison tables painful  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Table.ts` — **What:** A comparison table (e.g. CleanStart vs Competitor A vs Competitor B, 15 rows, 4 columns) requires the editor to navigate: outer Rows array → open Row n → inner Cells array → open Cell m → set type and value. Each row is a collapsed accordion showing only 'Row 01' with no label. Adding a column means editing every row manually. There is no bulk editing, no way to paste in a TSV/CSV structure, and no indication of how many columns a given row has vs how many headers are defined — column count drift is invisible until the page renders wrong. — **Recommendation:** Add a RowLabel component (similar to FaqRowLabel) that previews the first cell text so rows are scannable. Add a per-table `admin.description` warning 'Row cell count must match header count (currently N headers).' For a real fix, consider a custom UI component that renders the table as a 2D grid editor — this is a significant investment but a Table block that requires hand-editing nested arrays is one that editors avoid, defeating the purpose of having it.

### [MODERATE] All blocks always start expanded with no collapse-by-default — long pages become walls of fields  (authoring-ux · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/fields/BlocksField.tsx` — **What:** The custom BlocksField renders every block row fully expanded from the start. A page with 10–12 blocks (Hero + LogoCloud + FeatureGrid + Stats + CTA + Testimonial + FAQ + Pricing + FormBlock + CTA) produces a form that is hundreds of scrollable pixels tall with all fields visible simultaneously. There is no per-row collapse/expand toggle, no collapse-all button, and no saved-open-state. Compare to the FaqBulkPaste which went to the trouble of injecting a 'Collapse All' button into the FAQ array header — the Blocks field doesn't have even the native Payload array collapse behaviour. — **Recommendation:** Render each block row collapsed by default, showing only the row handle, index, block type label, and a summary derived from the first meaningful field (e.g. the headline for Hero/CTA, the feature count for FeatureGrid). Add expand/collapse chevron. Add 'Collapse all / Expand all' buttons to the block list header. This is the single change that would most improve day-to-day editing of complex pages.

### [MODERATE] Hero block missing 'layout' or 'alignment' variant — no way to express text-left vs text-center vs split  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Hero.ts` — **What:** The Hero block has eyebrow, headline, sub, two CTAs, and background (image/video/gradient/none) — but no variant or alignment field. Every hero will render identically regardless of whether the page calls for a centered product hero, a left-aligned split hero with product screenshot, or a narrow editorial hero. The background.media field appears only for image/video kinds, which is correct, but there is no field to control text alignment or whether the background image sits full-bleed behind text versus beside it in a split layout. — **Recommendation:** Add a 'layout' select: 'Centered (text over background)', 'Split left (text left, media right)', 'Split right (text right, media left)'. For split variants, add a 'mediaAlt' field if the media is a product screenshot rather than a decorative background. This is a common missing detail that forces developers to hard-code a layout per page.

### [MODERATE] Testimonial block cannot represent a carousel or multiple testimonials — no relationship to a Testimonials collection  (content-modeling · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Testimonial.ts` — **What:** The Testimonial block holds exactly one quote, person, role, company, logo, and avatar. Adding three testimonials to a page requires three separate Testimonial blocks, each completely independent. There is no reuse of testimonial content across pages — editing a quote requires finding every page where that testimonial appears and updating each one manually. Testimonial carousels (a standard B2B marketing pattern) require workarounds. — **Recommendation:** Either (a) add a 'testimonials' relationship field (hasMany: true, relationTo: 'testimonials') and create a Testimonials collection for reusable quotes, or (b) change the block to a 'Testimonials (group)' block with a repeating items array so at minimum a carousel of 3 is modellable in one block. Option (a) is the right content-modeling answer; option (b) is a quick step up from the current single-quote model. The current design conflates the block (page-level layout unit) with the data entity (a quote attributed to a person), which breaks content reuse.

### [MINOR] No Spacer / Divider block — vertical rhythm between sections requires workarounds  (completeness-gap · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/index.ts` — **What:** There is no way for an editor to insert a controlled vertical gap between blocks, or a decorative horizontal rule at the page level. The Section block's 'gap' select controls spacing between its children, but there is no top-level spacer for adjusting the rhythm between independently positioned blocks (e.g. between a Stats block and a CTA block). The RichText block can include an HR via the Lexical editor, but that is inline content, not a structural separator. — **Recommendation:** Add a Spacer block with a single 'size' select (sm/md/lg/xl) mapping to design token values, and optionally a boolean 'showDivider' for a visual HR. This is a 20-line file and would eliminate any custom CSS hacks developers add to compensate.

### [MINOR] No VideoBlock for standalone hero-width video — Embed block is iframe-only and lacks poster/autoplay controls  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/Embed.ts` — **What:** The Embed block handles YouTube/Vimeo/Loom iframes well but cannot represent a self-hosted .mp4 hero video (which the Hero block's `background.kind='video'` handles only as a background). There is no block for a prominent, centered, full-width product demo video with a poster image, play button, optional caption, and optional transcript link — a pattern almost every B2B SaaS marketing page needs in the 'How it works' section. — **Recommendation:** Consider adding a VideoBlock (distinct from Embed) that accepts a mediaUploadField for a self-hosted mp4, a poster image, a caption, and a boolean for autoplay-muted-loop (ambient background mode). Alternatively, extend the Embed block with a 'poster' upload field and 'autoplay' checkbox so the gap is narrower. Low urgency until a page actually needs it, but worth flagging now while the block library is still being formed.

### [MINOR] IntegrationLogos category taxonomy is hardcoded — adding a new category requires a code change  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/IntegrationLogos.ts` — **What:** The integration category select has seven hardcoded values (CI/CD, Container registry, Kubernetes, Cloud, Security, Observability, Other). When CleanStart adds support for a new integration category (e.g. Policy-as-Code, IDE plugins, Package managers), an editor cannot self-serve — they need a developer to add an option to the select, regenerate types, and deploy. — **Recommendation:** Create an IntegrationCategories collection (like NewsCategories / KnowledgeCategories) and change the category field to a relationship. This is consistent with how the rest of the CMS handles taxonomies and makes the block genuinely editor-owned. Trivial migration: the old string values become slug values in the new collection.

### [MINOR] The 'newTab' field on linkField is only shown when kind='url' — internal doc links cannot open in a new tab  (authoring-ux · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/fields/link.ts` — **What:** The newTab checkbox has an `admin.condition` that hides it when kind is not 'url'. Internal doc links and media links therefore always open in the same tab. This is a deliberate pattern for nav links (correct) but incorrect for CTA buttons on a page where the editor legitimately wants 'Download the PDF (new tab)' on a media link or 'View the webinar (new tab)' on a doc link. — **Recommendation:** Remove the condition from newTab so it applies to all three link kinds, or at minimum make it available for `kind='media'` (where opening in new tab is almost always correct for a file download). The current constraint is unnecessarily restrictive and will cause editors to reach for an external URL workaround for an internal media file just to get new-tab behavior.

### [MINOR] pageLayout sidebar field ('Narrow / Full-bleed') is underdocumented — editor has no way to know what it controls  (microcopy · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Pages.ts` — **What:** The pageLayout select has description: 'Container width and chrome.' This is insufficient to guide an editor deciding between Default, Narrow, and Full-bleed. An editor who sets 'Full-bleed' for a blog-style legal page or 'Narrow' for a pricing page will get an unexpected result, and the only way to understand the difference is to preview the page. — **Recommendation:** Improve the description to something like: 'Default: 1440px max-width container with side gutters (standard for most pages). Narrow: 720px max-width prose column (legal, about, text-heavy pages). Full-bleed: content fills the viewport edge-to-edge (landing pages where blocks own their own background). Affects all blocks on this page.'

### [QUESTION] No block-type description in picker distinguishes Section from direct top-level blocks  (authoring-ux · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/blocks/index.ts` — **What:** The Section block is first in the picker list per index.ts, but nothing in the picker UI explains the Section-block-children relationship to an editor who hasn't read the CLAUDE.md. Editors unfamiliar with the system may add a Hero inside a Section (valid but unusual) or may never use Section at all because direct top-level blocks also work. The design intent — Section is for layout composition, content blocks stand alone — is invisible from the UI. — **Recommendation:** Consider whether Section should appear at the very top of the picker with a distinct visual separator and description: 'Section — Layout container. Use to place two blocks side by side (e.g. text left, form right). For standalone full-width sections, add a block directly.' Alternatively, if Section is only needed for the two-column case, call it 'Two-column layout' to make its purpose self-evident from the name alone.

## Taxonomy, People, Galleries & Media

**Score: 6.5/10**

The taxonomy and media infrastructure is technically sound and shows real craft in specific areas — the media pipeline (SVG sanitisation, canonical filenames, collision avoidance, focal-point storage, PDF thumbnailing, clipboard paste, inline alt editing) is noticeably better than stock Payload and would make an editor's life easier. The Authors collection is well-modelled with thoughtful JSON-LD hooks and the LegacyBioViewer migration aid is a good temporary tool.

However, three separate category collections that share identical field shapes, stored separately purely for URL-namespace reasons, create real cognitive friction for editors who have to remember which taxonomy maps to which content type — particularly for cross-linking or future analytics. The AboutGalleries collection solves a real problem but is modelled as individual records rather than an ordered array, creating management overhead. The MediaPicker component exists but appears unused, the browse dialog only filters by filename (no MIME-type, folder, or date filter), and the lack of any bulk alt-text tooling or missing-alt dashboard is a gap for accessibility governance.

**Strengths**

- The Media collection's canonical-filename pipeline (slugify, short-hash suffix, context hint, collision check, junk-slug detection) produces clean, stable R2 keys that hold up post-ISR invalidation — this is meaningfully better than Payload's stock upload handling.
- Inline alt-text editing directly in the MediaField card (with an immediate PATCH and optimistic update) removes the back-and-forth to the Media list view that plagues every other Payload setup.
- SVG sanitisation via DOMPurify on upload is a security control most Payload projects skip entirely — the upstream XSS risk is real and it is correctly blocked here.
- The MediaSizeWarningField warning chip (>2 MB / >4000 px) surfaces Core Web Vitals risk at the right moment without blocking save — the correct balance between guidance and gate.
- AuthorCredibilityField's real-time published-content counter on the author sidebar is a genuinely useful editorial signal; most CMSes don't surface this without a custom dashboard query.
- The LegacyBioViewer escape-hatches the Webflow import gracefully: read-only, XSS-safe (pre-rendered, not innerHTML), auto-hides on new authors, and gives editors the original prose to copy from.
- The taxonomy reuse via buildTaxonomyFields keeps slug, SEO, parent cycle-guard, and redirect hooks consistent across all three category collections with zero duplication.
- PdfThumb using pdfjs-dist with dynamic import keeps the admin bundle clean while giving the editor a real first-page preview rather than a broken image icon.

**Findings**

### [MODERATE] Three separate category collections for one taxonomy pattern creates editorial confusion  (content-modeling · large)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Categories.ts, NewsCategories.ts, KnowledgeCategories.ts` — **What:** All three collections have identical field shapes (name, slug, description, icon, parent, SEO), identical hooks, and identical access rules — the only distinction is the URL path prefix used by the SERP preview. An editor managing the CMS sees three separate sidebar items under Taxonomies. When tagging a blog post they must remember 'Categories' is for blogs, 'News categories' for news, and 'Knowledge categories' for KB articles. There is no guard that prevents misuse (e.g. assigning a news category to a blog). The URL-namespace justification is real, but the cost is three mental models where one with a discriminator would do. The problem compounds as the content offering grows: adding podcast tags or event tracks means a fourth taxonomy collection. — **Recommendation:** Consolidate into a single Tags/Categories collection with a required 'scope' select field (values: 'blogs', 'news', 'knowledge', 'events', etc.). Filter the relationship pickers on each content collection to `scope: { equals: 'blogs' }`, etc. Taxonomy-page URL prefixes become a path function. This is a breaking schema change, so it requires a migration and a slug-redirect backfill — schedule for the next major cycle rather than doing it in-place. Until then, add `admin.description` to each collection explicitly naming which content type it belongs to.

### [MODERATE] About gallery item is modelled as individual records rather than an ordered list in a global  (content-modeling · medium)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/AboutGalleries.ts` — **What:** Each gallery image is a standalone collection record with its own draft/publish workflow, slug, SEO fields, and a manual displayOrder number. Reordering requires the editor to open each record, change the number, and save. The description field promises 'Drag to reorder in the list view (Phase D admin UX)' but no drag-and-drop sortable list is actually implemented — that comment is aspirational. Creating a new gallery item requires navigating away from the /about-us page entirely. The collection also has `versions: { drafts: true }`, meaning gallery items have their own draft/publish state — an editor could accidentally leave a gallery image in draft and wonder why it doesn't show on the page. A single-instance global with an array field (or an array block on the About page) would be far simpler. — **Recommendation:** Convert AboutGalleries to an array field on a dedicated AboutUs global (or on the Pages collection entry for /about-us). An array with an upload field + caption + imageLink per row supports drag-to-reorder natively in Payload's array UI and collapses the whole gallery into one save action. Remove `versions: drafts` from the current collection — gallery images don't need their own publish state. Effort is medium: create the global/array, write a migration script to populate it from existing records, verify the web app read path, then remove the old collection.

### [MODERATE] Media browse dialog searches only by filename — no MIME type, folder, or date filter  (authoring-ux · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/MediaBrowseDialog.tsx, MediaField/MediaField.tsx` — **What:** The thumbnail-grid picker in both the inline MediaField and the standalone MediaBrowseDialog supports only a single filename keyword search. With a growing asset library (blog hero images, author photos, guide PDFs, event banners all in one pool), editors searching for 'the PDF whitepaper from March' or 'all images in web/author' have no way to narrow by type, folder, or date. The browse also hard-caps at 24 results (`sort: -createdAt`) with no pagination — the 25th image is unreachable unless the editor happens to know part of its filename. The MediaPicker component (pickers/MediaPicker.tsx) has a 'Load more' button for pagination but appears to be unused anywhere in the codebase — dead code that solved the problem but wasn't wired up. — **Recommendation:** Add folder and mimeType filter chips to the browse UI (using the existing FOLDERS constant and MIME_LABELS map). Wire the MediaPicker's Load More / infinite-scroll pattern into MediaBrowseDialog as well, or simply add a page parameter to the existing fetch and show a 'Load more' button when `results.length === 24`. The folder filter alone would halve search time for most editorial workflows.

### [MODERATE] Authors collection is modelled as a full CV — excessive for a typical content team  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Authors.ts` — **What:** The Authors schema includes education (array), experience (array), skills (array), awards (array), location, and topicAreas — fields suited to a professional directory or a LinkedIn-like profile. For a security software company's blog, the typical author record needs name, photo, role, bioShort, bioLong, and social links. The structured experience/education/skills/awards arrays add 4 separate array widgets to an already-long form. The JSON-LD use case (Person.knowsAbout, Person.alumniOf) is real, but only if editors actually fill these fields. In practice, arrays with zero or one row are just noise. The LegacyBioViewer exists specifically because the Webflow import left structured bio data that editors haven't migrated yet — which suggests editors are not engaging with these fields. — **Recommendation:** Collapse education, experience, skills, and awards into a single optional freeform textarea called 'Extended bio (structured data)' with `admin.description` explaining it maps to JSON-LD. Alternatively, keep them but move the entire block into a collapsible group ('Extended profile / JSON-LD') that defaults closed so the main authoring surface stays clean. The LegacyBioViewer can also be deleted once the Webflow migration is declared complete.

### [MODERATE] No missing-alt-text dashboard or bulk-fix tooling for the media library  (completeness-gap · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Media.ts` — **What:** The collection enforces alt text at upload time for non-decorative images, which is correct. But there is no way for an editor or content manager to see all media records that currently have empty alt text (e.g. files uploaded before the validation was tightened, or PDFs that got through). The list view shows an 'alt' column but there is no pre-built filter for 'alt is empty AND decorative is false'. This is a real accessibility governance gap — WCAG auditors and lawyers care about deployed pages missing alt text, not the CMS validation state. — **Recommendation:** Add a saved filter in the admin list view, or a dedicated dashboard widget, that surfaces media items where alt is null/empty and decorative is false. Payload's list view supports defaultColumns and the where operator — a simple URL like `/admin/collections/media?where[alt][exists]=false` could be surfaced as a 'Fix missing alt' shortcut link in the Dashboard or the Media list header.

### [MINOR] MediaPicker component is dead code — exists but is never imported or used  (value-vs-complexity · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/pickers/MediaPicker.tsx` — **What:** MediaPicker.tsx (Wave 6) is a well-built Drawer-based media picker with pagination and filename search. Nothing imports it — no grep hit in the CMS source outside its own file and a style comment. It duplicates the browse-dialog pattern already implemented inside MediaField and MediaBrowseDialog. The component is not technically wrong, but it adds maintenance surface with no payoff. — **Recommendation:** Either (a) wire MediaPicker in as the canonical picker replacing the inline browse-dialog in MediaField and MediaBrowseDialog — giving the system one coherent picker implementation — or (b) delete it. The Drawer-based pattern is superior UX (more screen area, standard focus trap) but integrating it requires touching both MediaField and MediaBrowseDialog. If resources are tight, delete the dead code now and revisit when refactoring the browse experience.

### [MINOR] Category icon field uses a raw Payload upload relationship — no focal-point, no alt, no size hints  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/lib/build-taxonomy-fields.ts` — **What:** The icon field is defined as `type: 'upload', relationTo: 'media'` — the stock Payload upload picker, not the custom MediaField with inline alt editing, drag-and-drop, and browse. When an editor sets a category icon they get the raw Payload relationship picker (a text input with search), not the polished card UI the rest of the system uses. The icon also has no alt text at the category level (the alt lives on the media record), no enforced size guidance (should be SVG at 24×24 or a raster at 2x), and no help text. In practice, editors are likely to upload arbitrary PNGs. — **Recommendation:** Replace the raw upload field with `mediaUploadField({ name: 'icon', folderHint: 'web/general', accept: ['image/svg+xml', 'image/png', 'image/webp'] })` so the custom MediaField chrome is used consistently. Add `admin.description: 'SVG preferred; max 64×64 px. Used in category chips and nav filter pills.'` The accept restriction steers editors away from uploading a 2MB JPEG as an icon.

### [MINOR] Categories have no color/accent field — limits front-end differentiation  (content-modeling · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/lib/build-taxonomy-fields.ts` — **What:** The taxonomy field set has name, slug, description, icon, and parent — but no color or accent field. Category listing pages, filter chips, and content cards commonly use a per-category accent color to aid visual scanning (Contentful, Sanity, and most modern editorial CMSes include this). Without it, front-end developers must either hardcode a color map or all categories look identical. The blog listing page and knowledge hub index would benefit from this signal. — **Recommendation:** Add an optional text field named accentColor with `admin.description: 'CSS color token or hex value (e.g. #2563eb). Used for category chip backgrounds and article card accents. Leave blank for the default grey.'` A free-text field is preferable to a color picker widget for a design-system-governed site — editors should be referencing token names, not picking arbitrary hues.

### [MINOR] Draft/versions enabled on About gallery items adds unnecessary publish-state complexity  (authoring-ux · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/AboutGalleries.ts` — **What:** Gallery items have `versions: { drafts: true }` and the defaultColumns list shows `_status`. This means an editor uploading a team photo can accidentally leave it in draft and the /about-us page will silently omit it. The status column adds cognitive overhead (draft? published? for a gallery image?) to what should be a simple ordered list. The /about-us page is a single non-URL-addressed destination; it doesn't need per-image publishing workflow. — **Recommendation:** Remove `versions: { drafts: true }` from AboutGalleries. Gallery items should just be records that are either present or deleted. If staging visibility is needed, gate the entire /about-us page read on the site-wide publish state rather than per-image. This is the same pattern used by Footer nav items — no one gives nav items a draft/publish status.

### [MINOR] Filename rename in MediaField uses PATCH rather than the dedicated /rename endpoint  (authoring-ux · trivial)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/admin/components/MediaField/MediaField.tsx` — **What:** MediaField.tsx's onSaveFilename sends a bare PATCH to `/api/media/:id` with `{ filename: next }`. The Media.ts collection has a rejectFilenameRename beforeChange hook that explicitly blocks this path with a clear error message: 'Filename rename is not supported after upload. Detach the file and re-upload to change the name.' Meanwhile MediaSelfChrome.tsx (the edit-view chrome) correctly uses the `/api/media/:id/rename` endpoint which does the proper R2 copy-then-delete. The MediaField's rename will always fail silently or surface Payload's generic validation error. The rename UI in MediaField is misleading — it implies the action works but it will be rejected. — **Recommendation:** Either (a) update MediaField's onSaveFilename to call `/api/media/:id/rename` (matching MediaSelfChrome), or (b) remove the rename affordance from MediaField entirely — editing a media record's filename from within a blog post editor is an unusual workflow and removing it simplifies the component. Option (b) is lower risk.

### [QUESTION] topicAreas on Authors uses an array of free-text strings — not linked to the taxonomy system  (content-modeling · small)
**Where:** `apps/cms/src/payload/collections/Authors.ts` — **What:** The topicAreas field is described as powering Person JSON-LD knowsAbout and 'more from this author on X' suggestions, but is modelled as a free-text array (array of `{ topic: text }`). This means the values are disconnected from the Categories/KnowledgeCategories taxonomy. An author might have 'SBOM' as a topic area while the blog category is 'Software Bill of Materials' — no relationship between the two, no way to auto-filter blog posts by author expertise taxonomy, and the JSON-LD knowsAbout is whatever free text the editor typed. Should this be a has-many relationship to the categories taxonomy instead? — **Recommendation:** Decide: are topicAreas meant to be free-form expertise keywords (fine for JSON-LD) or editorial taxonomy tags (requiring a relationship)? If the former, keep as-is but add `admin.description` clarifying they are independent of blog categories. If the latter, change to a relationship field pointing to categories with hasMany: true. The current silence in the codebase (no UI surface using topicAreas for filtering) suggests this field is not yet rendering on the web app — confirm before migrating.
