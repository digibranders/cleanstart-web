As reported

28 core pages missing lastmod Medium No action
As reported

Listed in the Medium action plan: "Fix the 28 core pages missing lastmod."

Verified — correct by design
DecisionDo nothing — and specifically, do not add dates. Acting on this would make crawling worse.

Confirmed: 524 <loc>, 496 <lastmod>. The gap is deliberate and documented in code — lastmod is emitted only where accurate, from CMS timestamps. The 29 static routes have no meaningful document timestamp.

Google ignores, and learns to distrust, inaccurate lastmod. Synthesising build dates would degrade a working crawl signal.

Homepage canonical omits trailing slash Low No action
As reported

"Canonical is https://www.cleanstart.com (no slash) while the sitemap and served URL are .../ (with slash). Google normalizes this in practice but it's the only page where the mismatch occurs."

Verified — not fixable in app code
DecisionDo nothing. Already attempted and proven impossible.

The observation is accurate; the recommendation is not actionable. Next.js's metadata resolver collapses any root-path canonical to the bare origin unless trailingSlash is enabled — and this site strips trailing slashes everywhere by deliberate policy.

We implemented the fix, proved against a production build that the rendered tag was unchanged, and reverted. A regression test now pins the behaviour so the dead end isn't re-attempted.

resolve-url.js — resolveAbsoluteUrlWithPathname()
result.pathname === '/' ? result.origin : result.href

As reported

5. Two notes on method

The Search Console integration did not produce data

The Data Sources table lists Search Console Search Analytics, the URL Inspection API, CrUX and PageSpeed Insights. Not one metric from any of them appears in the report's sixteen pages — no clicks, impressions, CTR or average position; no index-coverage counts. The report concedes in its Performance section that no Google API key was configured.

This does not make the findings wrong — the crawl-based observations largely held up. It does mean this is a crawl plus analysis rather than a data-backed audit, and the claims that most need data are exactly the ones lacking it: cannibalization, ranking position, and indexation. The live property is connected; a re-run against it would settle those.

Where the false positives came from

Most refuted findings share one pattern: absence of evidence at an assumed location was read as absence of the feature. IndexNow was checked at two conventional paths rather than the one the protocol specifies. Missing lastmod was read as an omission rather than a documented policy.

The author finding is a different and more interesting case, and worth separating from the others. Its stated claim is wrong — 92% of posts do carry an author — but it was wrong in a useful way. The audit sampled the commercially important pages, and those are exactly where the 8% gap sits. A random sample would likely have missed it entirely. We only found the real shape of it by measuring all 73 posts instead of arguing from a sample of four.

Naming these matters because two of the recommendations would have made things actively worse: synthesising lastmod dates degrades a crawl signal Google distrusts when inaccurate, and the site-wide schema edit would have churned correct code while leaving all six real gaps untouched.

7. Where this leaves things

The audit is substantially sound and worth acting on. Its content and on-page sections in particular are specific, well-evidenced and correctly prioritised — the two Critical sourcing findings and the free-tier CTA observation are genuinely valuable, and nothing here softens them.

What changed after verification was the shape of the work, not its volume — and the engineering share has now been worked through. Nine recommendations should not be actioned as written, six items are shipped and live, and two are deferred with stated reasons. Roughly twenty-eight findings — the substantive majority — are content and information-architecture work, and that is where the remaining twenty-eight points of headroom sit.

Two things are worth carrying forward from how this went. The CSP burn-in earned its keep: reviewing before enforcing caught a change that would have silently disabled bot protection on every lead form. But a second breakage — blocked YouTube thumbnails — surfaced only after enforcement, on pages that had not been swept. Both fixes are live; the lesson is that the sweep should be exhaustive before the switch, not after.

Recommended next: run the content programme in §2, and re-run the audit with Search Console actually connected. That last step matters most — the findings that most need data to act on safely are the ones currently asserted without it.

Appendix A — The earlier "empty loading shells" diagnosis

Because those recommendations are concrete and two of them carry real risk, each is checked here individually. The symptom it reported was real. Every technical claim it made about the cause was wrong, and none of its proposed fixes should be implemented.

DecisionDo not implement. It solves a problem we do not have, and introduces several we do not want.

The content is already in the HTML, so there is nothing for a pre-renderer to add — it would spend money and add a single point of failure in front of the highest-value pages to duplicate work already done.

It also branches the site's output on user-agent. Google deprecated dynamic rendering as a recommended pattern precisely because these setups drift, and once the bot and human responses diverge — through a stale cache, a quota limit, or an outage — the result is serving search engines different content from users.
